Keith Strini and Derek Tracy have joined the CABE core team, alongside Hugo Landau, Andrés Vega, and Ryan Hurst.

Both have been involved in the work for some time, and both bring experience that has already shaped CABE.

## Keith Strini

Keith was involved in some of the early thinking that led to the project and has continued to push on federation, coalition operation, independent key custody, disconnected environments, and interoperability between separately operated systems.

That perspective comes from a long history working on mission systems and distributed environments. His background includes building NGA GeoInt Services, serving as principal engineer for Kessel Run’s Global AOC operational environment across five geographies, and serving as principal engineer for Kobayashi Maru’s Global Space Operations Center environment spanning FVEY partners. His work has also included BESPIN, the Army Futures Command software factory, Navy PEO C4I modernization, and DTRA/JSTO.

Keith has helped sharpen the project’s thinking around federation and operational constraints, particularly where multiple organizations need to exchange information while retaining control over their own infrastructure, policy, and keys.

## Derek Tracy

Derek brings a similarly deep background from the software and platform side. A U.S. Marine Corps veteran, he is a Mission Matter Expert at Dell Technologies working with Department of Defense and intelligence community customers.

He has helped architect and build software factories across DoD, including Kessel Run, Kobayashi Maru, the Army Software Factory, and the USMC Software Factory. He also led the effort to establish Kessel Run’s enduring continuous ATO process, later served as an embedded platform portfolio manager for the Army Software Factory, and has architected and accredited multitenant environments across NIPR, SIPR, and JWICS.

With CABE, Derek has spent more time pressure-testing the implementation side: integration paths, test tooling, policy outcomes, interfaces, and the places where a specification that looks straightforward on paper becomes less straightforward once someone actually tries to build against it.

## Why now

There is useful shared history here. Keith and Derek have both worked around Kessel Run and Kobayashi Maru, among other defense modernization efforts, and both have spent years dealing with many of the same problems CABE is intended to address: independently operated systems, multiple security domains, coalition boundaries, accreditation, intermittent connectivity, and software that still has to work once it leaves the architecture diagram.

Both have already been participating in the project in those roles. Adding them to the core team makes that responsibility explicit.

The current CABE core team is now Hugo Landau, Andrés Vega, Ryan Hurst, Keith Strini, and Derek Tracy.

The governance model will continue to evolve as more people implement, deploy, and contribute to CABE. The project should be shaped by more than the people who wrote its first version.
